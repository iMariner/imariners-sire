SIRE 2.0 in 5 minutes

SIRE 2.0 replaced the old VIQ7 inspection in September 2024. The big change is simple: the inspector no longer just checks paperwork. They watch the person who does the job show it and explain it. If you know your own job and your company procedure, you are already most of the way there.

01

The office fills the PIQ

Before the visit the company answers a pre-inspection questionnaire (PIQ) and the HVPQ about the ship.

02

A question set is built for your ship

The system picks questions to suit your ship. Core ones every time. Rotational 1 about every 3rd or 4th inspection, Rotational 2 about every 6th.

03

The inspector walks the ship

Area by area: documents, bridge, decks, manifold, CCR, engine room. Answers go straight into a tablet.

04

You are asked directly

The officer or rating who does the task is asked to show it. Not the Master answering for everyone.

05

Each answer is graded

Equipment, procedure and people are each judged. Negative observations say which one failed and why.

How your answer is graded

Each question has up to three parts. The question card below tells you which apply.

EquipmentNot as expectedAs expected
ProcedureNot as expectedLargely as expectedAs expected
PeopleNot as expectedLargely as expectedAs expectedExceeded expectation

Only the people part can earn a positive grade. A confident crew who know their job can lift the whole report.

On the day: do

  • Show the task, not just the record. Walk them to the equipment.
  • Open the SMS procedure and point to the step you follow.
  • Say "let me check the procedure" when unsure. That is a good answer.
  • Use your own words. Short and honest beats long and perfect.
  • Tell the truth about a defect and show how it is reported and tracked.

On the day: don't

  • Guess or invent an answer. A wrong answer becomes a human finding.
  • Let a senior answer for you when you were the one asked.
  • Fill a record on the spot to make it look complete.
  • Argue. Explain calmly, and ask what they expected to see.
  • Panic over one finding. One observation is not a failed ship.

      Questions seafarers ask

      SIRE 2.0 FAQ

      Will the inspector ask me all of these questions?

      No. Your ship gets a set picked from the library: all core questions that apply to her type, plus a rotating share of the rest. Rotational 1 questions come up roughly every third or fourth inspection, Rotational 2 roughly every sixth. Study in that order: core first, then Rotational 1, then Rotational 2. The list here shows everything you could be asked.

      I am a junior officer. Will they really ask me and not the Master?

      Yes, that is the point of SIRE 2.0. The inspector wants to see the person who does the task show how it is done. That is good news: you know your own job better than anyone.

      What if I don't know an answer?

      Say so, then show where the answer is: the SMS procedure, the manual or the record. Knowing where to look is part of being competent. Guessing wrongly is what turns into a human factor observation.

      Does one negative observation mean the ship fails?

      No. SIRE is not pass or fail. Oil majors and charterers read the full report and how the company responds. Each observation is closed out by the operator with a corrective action.

      Our company procedure says something different from this page. Which is right?

      Your company SMS. The inspector checks whether you follow your own procedures. Use this page to understand what is being looked for, then check how your SMS covers it.

      Who decides which rank answers which question?

      The rank tags here follow normal tanker practice and the wording of each question. Duties vary by company, so if your ship splits jobs differently, use the search and the "Also show questions I may be asked" box.
